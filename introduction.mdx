FittingMe lets a shopper see a garment on a photo of themselves without leaving your client's product page. You add an entry point to the storefront; FittingMe provides the try-on drawer, photo and consent steps, generation, and result.

**Start with one product and the FittingMe loader.** The loader is a JavaScript file that creates and manages an isolated iframe. You do not need to build the try-on screens or call the generation API yourself.

## The shopper experience

1. The shopper opens try-on from a product page.
2. FittingMe asks for the required consent and photo. On desktop, the shopper can use the phone handoff; the available photo options depend on the device and session.
3. FittingMe generates and displays the try-on result.
4. The shopper closes the drawer and continues shopping on your site.

An eligible product, an opened drawer, and a completed try-on are different milestones. Your integration can observe availability and opening through [public events](/reference/events); completion is reported only when a completed-result drawer closes, and is not a unique-generation count.

## Who owns what

| You and your client | FittingMe |
| --- | --- |
| Storefront access, installation, and page layout | Hosted loader, widget, and try-on services |
| Correct product identifier and garment image | Photo capture and try-on generation |
| Product and variant selection, cart, and checkout | Consent screens and journey state |
| Storefront security policy and analytics consent | Public integration events and error responses |
| Testing on the client's actual storefront | Access provisioning and assisted catalogue onboarding |

The widget does not own your product selector, cart, or checkout. A try-on completion is not an order or a size recommendation. This guide covers virtual try-on.

## Does this fit your storefront?

The recommended integration needs a page where you can add an HTML container and load an external script. Your security policy must allow the FittingMe iframe. Server-rendered pages and browser-rendered applications can use the loader; dynamic applications also need explicit cleanup when the product changes.

Use [direct embedding](/integration/direct-embed) only when you need to own the iframe integration yourself. That approach also makes you responsible for message validation, layout, scrolling, focus, and browser history.
