Each embed needs `data-product-id`. It can use an image you supply or a product already prepared in FittingMe's catalogue. Start with the supplied-image approach unless FittingMe has confirmed your catalogue is ready.

| Approach | Supply | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplied image | Product identifier and `data-garment-image-url` | You can provide the correct garment image from the storefront. |
| Prepared catalogue | Product identifier; omit `data-garment-image-url` | FittingMe has onboarded the product and confirmed try-on availability. |

## Supply an image

1. Choose a stable product identifier from your storefront. Keep it consistent between the displayed product, the embed, and your own analytics.
2. Select the garment image for that product or colour. Confirm it represents the item the shopper sees.
3. Verify that the URL meets the requirements below and is retrievable by FittingMe's services.
4. Set `data-product-id` and `data-garment-image-url` before loading the script. Use the [quickstart](/start/quickstart) as the complete example.
5. Generate a result and visually verify the garment. Loading the source image in your browser does not establish server-side accessibility.

### Image URL requirements

- The downloaded image must decode as **JPEG or PNG**, with at most **20 MiB (20,971,520 bytes)**. WebP, AVIF, GIF, and SVG are not accepted garment formats. Renaming a file does not change its format.
- Width and height must each be at most **8,192 pixels**. Malformed images and images that exceed decoding limits are rejected even when their download is under 20 MiB.
- FittingMe follows at most **three redirects**. Prefer a direct image response; each redirect and destination must remain publicly accessible and pass the service's URL checks.
- Absolute HTTPS URL, with a hostname.
- At most 1,024 Unicode characters, both before and after URL normalisation.
- No embedded username or password, fragment (`#...`), whitespace, or control characters.
- Reachable without a shopper's cookies, login, or a private network connection.

Use a stable media URL. A URL that expires during a shopper's journey can interrupt generation. Keep image credentials and private customer data out of query strings.

These are garment-image requirements, separate from the shopper-photo formats accepted in the upload screen. Configure an image CDN to return JPEG or PNG consistently; automatic WebP/AVIF negotiation can make a working browser image unsuitable for generation.

An eligible bootstrap does not download and validate the garment. An unsupported image can fail only after **Start**, currently with a generic temporary-unavailability message and no dedicated public loader error event. Check the actual response bytes, size, and redirects before retrying; eligibility is not proof that generation will succeed.

An explicitly supplied invalid or inaccessible image is an error. FittingMe does not silently replace it with a catalogue image. To use catalogue lookup, **omit the image attribute entirely**; an empty attribute is invalid.

## Use a prepared catalogue

1. Arrange catalogue onboarding with your FittingMe contact. Agree the product identifiers and source images using your storefront's product data.
2. Obtain confirmation that the intended products are ready for try-on in the environment you will use.
3. Pass the exact agreed identifier in `data-product-id` and omit `data-garment-image-url`.
4. Test both a confirmed eligible product and a product that should not offer try-on.

You do not need to implement catalogue administration endpoints in the browser. Read the embed's [bootstrap outcome](/reference/events#bootstrap-outcome) to determine whether it can open. A temporary failure is different from a product being ineligible.

## Change products deliberately

Product and image inputs belong to one embed for its lifetime. Editing attributes on an already-executed script does not reconfigure it. Follow [product and variant changes](/integration/product-changes) when navigation or a colour selection changes the garment.
