Use your client's established FittingMe support contact. If you do not have one, ask your client to introduce its onboarding contact, or email [contact@fittingme.ai](mailto:contact@fittingme.ai), the address listed on the [FittingMe website](https://fittingme.ai/).

Contact FittingMe when you need retailer access, entitlement, storefront framing approval, assisted catalogue onboarding, a confirmed service-side failure, or help after the relevant [symptom checks](/troubleshooting/symptoms).

## Prepare a useful report

Include:

1. The retailer name, environment, and affected storefront hostname.
2. Date, time, and time zone of the failure.
3. Browser/version, operating system, and whether you used a physical phone or desktop emulation.
4. Whether you used the loader or a direct embed, and a supplied image or prepared catalogue.
5. Exact reproduction steps, what you expected, and what happened.
6. The loader's bootstrap outcome or command verdict, plus the failing response's HTTP status and safe error `code` where available.
7. Whether it affects one product, one browser, or all attempts; note the last known working storefront build if relevant.

Provide a minimal example with placeholders for credentials. Share an affected public product page only when your client permits it. Keep private preview links in the agreed support channel.

## Redact before sharing

Remove full retailer keys, cookies, authentication headers, session identifiers, capture/QR URLs, shopper photos, result URLs, and personal information. Browser network exports and screenshots can contain these values even when the visible error text does not.

Do not open a Problem Details `type` URI expecting a support page. It is an error identifier; provide the safe `code` instead.

## Report the impact clearly

State whether normal browsing, cart, or checkout is affected. If shoppers cannot use the storefront, follow your prepared [disable procedure](/maintenance/updates#disable-the-entry-point) while investigating. Do not send repeated generation requests as a substitute for diagnosing a persistent failure.
